# 12 — Upwork Offer

C'est le meilleur canal de cette prestation. Un audit correspond exactement à ce qu'un client Upwork sait acheter : un livrable défini, un prix fixe, un regard extérieur qualifié. Et le budget moyen y est deux à quatre fois celui de Fiverr pour le même travail.

---

## A. Profil

**Titre**
> Shopify CRO Audit & Conversion Consultant — Evidence-Based Reports

**Overview**

```
I audit Shopify stores and tell you, in order, what to fix first.

Not a checklist with your logo on it. Every finding in my reports cites a
screenshot, a number from your analytics, or a line of your theme code — and
every recommendation is costed in hours, so the report is something you can
actually decide from.

What that looks like in practice
• I go through your store as a buyer would: real phone, real mobile data,
  screen recorded. Before I look at any analytics, so my impressions aren't
  contaminated by what I already know.
• I check every observation against your theme's code. When I write "your
  reviews widget shifts the page 1.8s after load", I can show you the file.
• I read your funnel: where people leave, on which device, from which source.
• I audit your tracking first. If your data is wrong, every decision you make
  from it is wrong — and this is broken more often than anyone expects.
• I compare you to 3-5 competitors on the same criteria, with the same
  screenshots, so the comparison means something.
• I score 10 dimensions out of 100 and show the calculation, so you can argue
  with any individual finding rather than with a number I made up.

What I won't do
• Quote you a conversion increase. Nobody honest can predict that
• Pad the report. 10-12 readable pages beats 80 unread ones
• Recommend a roadmap you can't afford — I ask your budget first, and calibrate
• Bill you for things you can fix yourself in your admin in five minutes.
  Those get their own section

I'm also a Shopify developer, which matters for two reasons: I can verify my
claims in the code, and I know what things actually cost to build because I
build them.

Tools: Shopify Analytics, GA4, Search Console, Web Performance dashboards,
Lighthouse, Chrome DevTools, Shopify CLI, Liquid.
```

**Compétences :** Shopify, Conversion Rate Optimization, UX Research, Web Analytics, Google Analytics, SEO Audit, Ecommerce, User Experience, Liquid

**Mots-clés :** shopify audit, cro audit, conversion rate optimization, ecommerce audit, ux audit, shopify consultant, conversion report

## B. Structure de proposal

```
1. Une observation réelle sur SON store, faite avant d'écrire     ← décisif
2. La question que cette observation soulève
3. Ta méthode, en 4 lignes, avec ce qui te distingue
4. Ce que tu ne feras pas
5. Une question qui montre que tu penses à son business
6. Prix, délai, livrables
```

Ne commence jamais par ton expérience. Une observation vaut dix lignes de biographie.

## C. Proposal courte

```
Hi {{Name}},

I went through {{store}} on my phone before writing this. Two things stood out:

On your product pages, the price sits below the fold on mobile — I had to scroll
about one and a half screens before I could see what the product costs, and two
and a half before I could add it to the cart. If a meaningful share of your
traffic is mobile and lands directly on product pages from ads, that's a lot of
people being asked to work before they have a reason to.

Second, your search returns nothing for {{terme évident du catalogue}}, though
you clearly sell it.

Neither of these is a diagnosis yet — I'd need your funnel data to know whether
they matter or whether your drop-off is somewhere else entirely. That's what the
audit is for.

How I work: recorded buyer journey on a real device, funnel analysis, the full
store screen by screen, technical checks (performance, SEO, accessibility,
tracking), 3-5 competitors on the same criteria. Every finding gets a screenshot
or a number, and an estimate in hours. Score out of 100 with the calculation
shown.

I won't quote you a conversion increase, and I'll flag anything you can fix
yourself.

One question: where does your paid traffic land — homepage or product pages?
That changes which half of the store I should spend my time on.

{{Price}} — {{days}} business days, including a 45-minute call to walk through it.

{{Name}}
```

## D. Proposal longue (budgets > 1 500 $)

```
Hi {{Name}},

Notes from twenty minutes on {{store}} before I wrote this.

WHAT I CAN SEE FROM OUTSIDE
{{3-5 observations vérifiables, chacune avec ce qui la rend notable}}

I want to be clear that none of these are conclusions. They're the kind of thing
that turns out to matter or not depending on your funnel data — which is exactly
why the audit starts there rather than with opinions about your design.

SCOPE
1. Data first — funnel by step, conversion by device, traffic sources, internal
   search terms, 90 days vs previous 90. Plus a reliability check: does GA4
   agree with Shopify? Broken tracking is common and makes every other number
   meaningless.
2. Buyer journey — real device, real mobile connection, screen recorded, before
   I've formed technical opinions. This recording is part of the deliverable.
3. Store audit — 29 areas, mobile and desktop, every finding with a named
   screenshot.
4. Technical — Core Web Vitals from your real visitor data, technical SEO,
   structured data, accessibility, app load, theme code verification.
5. Competitors — {{n}} stores on the same criteria, same screenshots, plus what
   nobody in your category does.
6. Scoring, prioritisation, roadmap costed in hours and calibrated to your
   implementation budget.

DELIVERABLES
Main report (10-12 pages), 6 annexes, a two-page prioritized roadmap with an
owner and an hour estimate per line, and a 60-minute recorded walkthrough call.

WHAT I WILL NOT DO
Quote you a conversion percentage. Recommend a roadmap beyond your budget.
Bill you for items you can fix in your admin — those get their own section.
Claim certainty where I have a hypothesis; findings are labelled by evidence type.

QUESTIONS
1. Roughly what budget do you have for implementing the findings? Not about my
   price — about making the roadmap usable.
2. Who would do the work: me, your agency, or your team?
3. What questions do customers ask you most before buying?
4. Has anything changed recently — theme update, app, new traffic source?

{{Price}} — {{days}} business days.

{{Name}}
```

La question 3 est celle qui impressionne. Personne ne la pose, et elle montre que tu sais où sont les vrais constats.

## E. Qualification

| Question | Pourquoi |
|---|---|
| Combien de sessions par mois ? | Sous ~1 000, l'audit est spéculatif. Le dire |
| Taux de conversion actuel ? | S'il est déjà bon, le sujet est ailleurs |
| Accès analytics possible ? | Détermine la valeur réelle du livrable |
| Budget d'implémentation ? | Calibre la feuille de route |
| Qui exécutera ? | Détermine le niveau de précision d'écriture |
| Refonte prévue ? | Change complètement les recommandations |
| Un audit a-t-il déjà été fait ? | Ne pas répéter ce qui a déjà été dit |

---

## F. Red flags

| Signal | Réaction |
|---|---|
| « Garantissez-vous +X % de conversion ? » | Expliquer pourquoi personne ne peut. Si le client insiste, passer |
| Moins de 500 sessions/mois | Dire honnêtement que le levier est le trafic |
| Refus de tout accès aux données | Accepter uniquement en audit d'interface, avec la limite écrite en page 1 |
| « On veut un audit pour convaincre notre direction » | Le rapport doit être défendable devant quelqu'un que tu ne verras pas. Prix plus élevé, écriture plus rigoureuse |
| Budget d'audit élevé, budget d'implémentation nul | L'audit ne servira à rien. Le dire |
| « Regardez juste vite fait et dites-nous ce qui ne va pas » | Ce n'est pas un audit, c'est un avis gratuit. Refuser ou facturer une heure de consultation |
| Boutique en pré-lancement | Ce n'est pas de l'audit, c'est du conseil de conception. Autre prestation, autre prix |

## G. La conversion vers l'implémentation

C'est là qu'est la vraie rentabilité de cette phase. Sur Upwork, la mécanique :

1. **Livre l'audit avec la restitution.** Jamais sans appel.
2. **Pendant l'appel**, note ce que le client dit vouloir faire en premier. C'est ton devis.
3. **Sous 48 h**, envoie une proposition d'implémentation du Lot 1, reprenant ses mots.
4. **Propose un nouveau contrat** plutôt qu'un avenant : c'est plus clair pour lui, et ça produit un second avis public.
5. **Le ré-audit à 3 mois** est ta relance naturelle, et il se vend seul quand les chiffres ont bougé.

Formulation en fin de restitution :

> Je vous envoie le rapport ajusté avec ce dont on vient de parler. Vous m'avez dit vouloir commencer par {{X}} et {{Y}} : je vous prépare un devis pour ce lot précis, sans engagement, sous 48 heures. Et que vous travailliez avec moi ou avec quelqu'un d'autre, la feuille de route est écrite pour être exécutable par n'importe quel développeur Shopify — c'est fait exprès.

La dernière phrase désamorce toute impression de dépendance, et c'est précisément ce qui fait signer.
